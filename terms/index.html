If you host a keepsake

Hosting means you are the one responsible for it. Specifically:

  • You decide who is invited. Share the code with the people you want, and understand that anyone holding the code can leave a memory.
  • You moderate it. You can delete any memory in your keepsake, at any time, for any reason. We expect you to use that when something has been left that would hurt the family.
  • You have the standing to gather these memories. If the keepsake honours someone who has died, you are a family member or you have the family's agreement. This is not a formality — it is the difference between a keepsake and a page about someone who never asked for one.
  • You handle the takedown requests you receive. If a guest asks you to remove what they left, remove it.

If you leave a memory

  • Put your real name, or the name the family would know you by. A keepsake full of anonymous entries is worth much less to them.
  • Write about what you actually remember. Don't put words in the mouth of someone who cannot correct you.
  • Don't use a keepsake to settle something. Grief makes room for a lot, but not for abuse, threats, harassment, or airing someone's private business.
  • Only add photos you took, or ones you are allowed to share.
  • The host of the keepsake can see and delete what you leave, and so can we if we have to.
